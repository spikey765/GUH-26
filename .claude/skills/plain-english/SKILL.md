name: plain-english
description: Rules for any prose meant for people, such as README, Devpost write-up, pitch script, slide text or UI copy. Use whenever writing or editing that kind of text.
---

Write like a sharp person talking, not like a chatbot.

Banned words: delve, tapestry, vibrant, crucial, comprehensive, meticulous, embark, robust, seamless, groundbreaking, leverage, synergy, transformative, paramount, multifaceted, myriad, cornerstone, reimagine, empower, catalyst, bolster, spearhead, invaluable, bustling, nestled, realm, showcase, foster, garner, interplay, enduring, pivotal, intricate, harness, unleash, revolutionize, elucidate, encompass, holistic, utilize, facilitate, nuanced, paradigm, poised, additionally, furthermore.

Banned phrases: "In today's...", "It is worth noting", "plays a crucial role", "serves as a testament", "in the realm of", "harness the power of", "the landscape of", "In order to", "Due to the fact that", "I hope this helps", "Great question".

Rules:
- Use "is" and "has", not "serves as" or "boasts".
- No significance claims ("a pivotal moment"). State the fact.
- No vague sources ("experts say"). Name the source or cut the claim.
- No sentences ending in "-ing" clauses such as "highlighting the need for...". Make it its own sentence.
- No "not just X, it's Y". No lists of three adjectives for rhythm.
- No em dashes. Use straight quotes. No emojis. Bold only where a reader must not miss it.
- Headings in sentence case.
- No generic closing lines. End on a specific fact or next step.
- Vary sentence length. Use numbers, names and dates. Contractions are fine.

UI copy for the persona: reading age 9 to 11, short sentences, pounds and dates rather than percentages where possible.

Before finishing, reread the text and fix anything above.
